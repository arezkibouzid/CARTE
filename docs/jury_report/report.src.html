CARTE — Constraint-Aware Routing with Trust Estimation

Convoy route planning from a scanned map, under constraints, with explained decisions and a priced doubt. Technical report for the jury · AMIAD hackathon · November 2026
What the application does. From the IGN 1:100,000 scan (and BD TOPO / OSM if available) it builds a road graph, then computes convoy routes with one cost formula that holds every constraint: hard limits remove roads, soft ones price them, and the model's own doubt about a road read from the image is priced too. Every route is explained segment by segment; orders (PDF or typed text) are read by a local, frozen language model and matched to 214 NATO doctrine rules. Routing runs on one CPU core in 0.14 s. Every figure below is measured on the hackathon sheet (runs in experiments/runs/).

3. How constraints are handled

R* = argminR ⊆ A Σe∈R base(e) · (1 + s·share<τ(e)) · (1 + λ·risk(e)) · Πj(1 + wj·φj(e)),   A = E \ (area ∪ zones ∪ vehicle ∪ earlier legs)
  • Hard (removed): outside the deployment area, zones to avoid (drawn or written), roads too narrow for the vehicle, the path of earlier legs of the mission (reopened only around the shared point if there is no other way).
  • Soft (priced): length or time (legal limits, 50 km/h in built-up areas, convoy speed cap), roads already used by an earlier convoy (deconfliction), low image confidence.
  • Uncertainty: risk(e) = mean of −ln(p/0.78) along the section, p the calibrated U-Net probability; λ = 0.5 chosen at the knee of the ablation (−9 % km off real roads for +0.5 % length). Applied only when the route relies on the scan alone.
  • Also: fixed or free waypoint order, round trip on a distinct return, k distinct itineraries with a minimum separation, several convoys with priorities.
Uncertainty strength λ: km of route off real roads (10 exercises). λ = 0.5 is the knee.
Network source ablation, 10 exercises: closeness to the BD TOPO route.

6. Civilian applications

The chain map image → road graph with confidence → constrained, explained routing does not depend on the military purpose: only the rule base, the vehicle list and the default criteria do.

  • Civil protection, fire and rescue: heavy vehicles or evacuations when OSM is incomplete or outdated; several convoys with deconfliction for staggered evacuations.
  • Humanitarian mapping: roads read from scanned maps where vector data is sparse, with the uncertainty map telling volunteers where to check first.
  • Exceptional transport: oversize loads (width, bridges) with the constrained itineraries; the doctrine CSV can be replaced by road regulations.
  • Historical maps and network maintenance: digitising old road networks; comparing an image-read network with a reference to flag missing or vanished roads.

Adapting to another map style: rasterise a vector reference onto the images, fine-tune from our weights (6–12k steps, 8–15 min on an H100; two sheets were enough here), recalibrate, re-check λ.

7. Limitations

  • One sheet of evidence: every routing figure comes from the 10 exercises of the hackathon sheet; λ and the thresholds should be re-measured elsewhere.
  • Vision handles fewer constraints: width, load class, height and road names are not read from a map; the rules that need them are flagged "to check". Scattered gaps in the read network break routes (5 % of sections removed: 73 % feasible).
  • Static world: no time, weather or moving threats; convoys planned in sequence, not timed at junctions.
  • Language model: optional and checked (ids, numbers, rule statuses, values in force), but the checks are heuristics; when a check fails, the app gives its deterministic answer instead.
  • Doctrine: 18 of 214 quotes not found automatically (mostly a scanned manual): flagged in the app; tables and figures of the manuals are not interpreted.
